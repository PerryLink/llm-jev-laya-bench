Each PDF's title block now carries all three DOIs and says, in its own language, that the
two papers are language versions of ONE piece of work rather than two papers, and that the
English text governs where they differ. That sentence is load-bearing: Zenodo has no
`is translation of` relation -- verified against its own vocabulary, 34 relation types,
`istranslationof` absent -- so the machine-readable link that would normally prevent a
duplicate-submission reading cannot exist, and prose in the PDF is the only thing left
carrying it. A reader who has the PDF and never sees the Zenodo page gets the same
information as one who does.

CITATION.cff keeps `doi` as the artifact's concept DOI, because that key means "the DOI of
THIS software or dataset". The two paper DOIs go under `identifiers` with descriptions
saying which is which. Putting a paper DOI in `doi` would make the artifact claim to be
the paper -- the same wrong-object attribution this repository keeps finding. Version was
stale at v1.0.1 when the artifact has been at v1.0.2; corrected.

(24 -> 59) that had been wrong since the check suite grew.

PDF sizes and hashes changed, so paper/pdf/README.md is updated. Both re-verified: A4 on
all 176 pages, no header or footer, full text, CJK intact.

Known cosmetic flaw, recorded rather than fixed: the DOI URLs wrap mid-number in the printed
PDFs, so copying one by hand can pick up a line break. The cause is `a { word-break:
break-all }`, which is the rule that keeps long URLs from overflowing A4 table cells -- the
97 pages have no overflow because of it. Trading a copy-paste annoyance for possible table
overflow is a bad trade in a print artifact, and the DOIs are correct in the text and on
every landing page.
