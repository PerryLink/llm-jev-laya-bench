## 0. Findings ordered by severity (detail in §1–§6)

1. **The battery disables the typed judge's only differentiated failure mode, and the paper's title claims that failure mode.** R5 §4.2 #5 caps state at 450 tokens; R5 §5.2 fixes token count across the horizon dial; R14's acceptance gate G1 rejects every item whose state would exceed 512. So `token_ratio(t)` is degenerate and `P(wrong | truncated)` is unestimable in the primary matrix, while R5 §0 titles the paper *"cost is not the constraint, **silent degradation** is"*. (V5 BLOCKER-1 reaches the same conclusion from the reproducibility side; §5.1 adds the fairness reading and the fix.)
2. **The anti-straw-man controls R16 declares mandatory are absent, and restoring them costs ≈$2 of replay.** No P1–P4 prompt band, no logit readout, no `uncertain`/charged-retry, no conjunction of batching × k=8. R16 §D.5 says all four of its controls are required and "any one alone is insufficient"; R5 keeps two. (§1, §3)
3. **The cost chapter rests on an assumed cache-hit rate that the design's own frozen serializer makes unattainable, and on a 120-output-token assumption that thinking mode invalidates.** At 85% hit + a 1,500-token reasoning budget, $0.10 buys **99** LLM judgments — fewer than the 120 checkpoints — so "money is definitively not the constraint" holds only in the non-thinking configuration R5 never names. (§2.3)
4. **The core calibration comparison is rigged in the typed judges' favour**: A1 returns `{label, prob}` — a top-label scalar — while R5 §4.3 #10 requires the typed judges to report full distributions. R16 §A.2.1 R3 explicitly gives the LLM arm `p_all` over every option; R5 never grants it. (§4.3)
5. **A1 is the floor for every claim, but its estimand is overstated by at least the self-judgment confound, and the "same question" guarantee is a snapshot guarantee, not a wire guarantee.** The fix (same LLM judging a *foreign* run's frozen snapshot) is free on the existing replay plane. (§4.1–§4.2)
6. **Five unjustified asymmetries move the headline** (escalation is typed-only; no typed batching arm; no in-domain adaptation for the LLM arm; order frozen to protect a statistic that is banned from use; latency timed under generator contention). (§5)

## 1. Q1 — Is the LLM arm a straw man?

**What R5 has.** A0 (free prose fused into the step), A1 (same LLM, same typed question, JSON `label` + `prob`), A5b (batch ≤8 checkpoints/call), A5c (self-consistency k=8 at `B_hi`). R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 1 keeps A5b/A5c and calls them "绝不可删" (never to be deleted), citing R15's argument and R13's constraint 22 ("a comparison in which one judge batches and the other does not is not a comparison").

**Credit where it is due.** On the two axes a reviewer most often attacks — *you never let the LLM batch* and *you never let the LLM sample more* — R5 is defended, and defended in the right way (A5b holds token cost constant while cutting round-trips 120 → 15; A5c is a genuine k=8 judge, "strictly stronger than A1", R15 §1.6).

**The straw man that remains.** R16 §A.7 prints twelve gates; R5 satisfies 1, 2, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12 and fails **3, 4, 5, 8, 9**:

| R16 gate | R16 requirement | R5 status |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | "Prompt band reported — P1–P4 (§A.2.3) with min/median/max, plus the typed-side band" | **Absent.** R5 contains no P1–P4 (verified by search). A1 is one frozen prompt |
| 4 | "Best LLM variant is the headline — Not the mean, not the laziest" | **Unimplementable** without a band |
| 5 | "Logit readout attempted — including parse-failure rate" | **Absent.** R5 §2 fact 10 records that `logprobs`/`top_logprobs` exist — and never assigns them to an arm |
| 8 | "Abstention available to all — `uncertain` / `insufficient` / low-confidence treated identically" | **Absent for the LLM arm.** R15 §0.4's question bank is `noul: {true,false}; choice: exactly 8; score: 5 levels` — there is no `uncertain` key anywhere in it. R5 §5.3 further pins A1 to JSON containing `label` + `prob` |
| 9 | "Calibration repair symmetric — same held-out split, same temperature-scaling budget per arm" | **Unstated.** R16 §A.2.2 rule 4 ("both sides get the same repair budget") does not appear in R5 |

**Name the missing configuration.** The strongest LLM judge this budget can buy is not any single existing arm; it is the **conjunction**:

> **A5\* — maximally-configured LLM judge** = best variant of the P1–P4 band (selected on the P5 calibration corpus before unblinding) × `p_all` full distribution + logit readout variant × batched ≤8 checkpoints/call × k=8 self-consistency × explicit `uncertain` key + one budget-charged re-ask × reasoning effort pinned and swept.

No arm in R5 §5.3 is this, and three of its six components (band, logit, `uncertain`+retry) do not exist anywhere in R5. A5b and A5c each supply one component; a reviewer who demands "the best-prompted, best-sampled, batched, abstention-capable LLM at the same money" is asking for the conjunction, and R16 §D.5 control 1 — the control R16 calls the "anti-straw-man instrument" — is exactly what is missing.

**The cost of the fix is the damning part.** Because R5 §5.4's measurement plane replays all judges on frozen snapshots, A5\* requires **no new generation**: 1,000 items × 4 prompt variants × 1 call = 4,000 judgment calls ≈ **$0.44** at R3's own per-call price; with k=8 on top, ≈$3.5. The design omits its most important fairness control to save single-digit dollars.

**The mirror-image straw man nobody in R5 flags.** The typed side is represented in the primary analysis by its *weakest* instance: A2 (Jev) is "**当前阻塞**（mock）" (R5 §5.3; my probe above confirms it is still a hash function), so the primary typed judge is A3 = a zero-shot 421M checkpoint whose own documentation says it is near chance on exactly this class of tasks (R2 §B.7: English 0.362 vs random 0.318 vs majority-class 0.461; *"Laya 是一个可快速特化的基座，不是零样本决策引擎"*). A design that let the strongest LLM configuration be absent *and* let the strongest typed instance be a mock is not rigged in one direction — it is rigged in both, and only one direction is audited in R5. R16 §0's symmetry command covers affordances; it does not cover **instance strength**, which is the same class of error.

**VERDICT: unsound** — the LLM arm is under-configured in four separable ways against R16's own printed gate list, and the missing configuration (A5\*) is nameable, affordably replayable, and required by R16 §D.5. **Minimal fix:** add one row to R5 §5.3, `A5*` as defined above, run on the measurement plane only; declare A5\* the headline LLM number and A1 the confound-control floor (not the headline), and state in the same table that A2 is mock-blocked so the typed side is currently represented by A3 alone.

## 2. Q2 — The dollar axis was demoted on the claim that money is not the binding constraint

### 2.1 The demotion re-derived (it survives, narrowly)

From R2 §A (off-peak: hit $0.003/M, miss $0.15/M, out $0.60/M) and R16 §B.2, with R15's token volumes (1,500 in, 120 out) and 85% hit:

* LLM structured judgment = 1275 × 0.003 + 225 × 0.15 + 120 × 0.60, all per 1M = **$0.00010958** → $0.10 buys **913** calls ≈ 7.6 per checkpoint.
* 0% hit = **$0.000297** → $0.10 buys **337** calls ≈ 2.8 per checkpoint.
* Laya (local, R15 §1.6) $0.000035–$0.000068/call; Jev $0.000063/call.

So at `B_lo = $0.10/run` **every** arm can afford ≥1 judgment per checkpoint with margin, and the "dose" axis is not dollar-limited. R3 §4's qualitative conclusion is correct **as a statement about marginal API token spend at 120 checkpoints**. I agree with the direction of the demotion.

### 2.2 One promoted "fact" does not reproduce, and one row has the wrong sign

R5 §2 elevates R3's crossover table to a design-shaping fact: *"成本交叉点 **2,100–7,100 token**，**随 horizon 消失**"* (crossover 2,100–7,100 tokens; vanishes with horizon), and R5's paper skeleton §6 promises *"Jev 交叉点随 horizon 移动"* as a result. I cannot reproduce it:

* Jev's marginal input price is flat: $0.042/M = **4.2e-8 $/token**, output free.
* The LLM's blended marginal input price is (1−h)·1.5e-7 + h·3e-9, which is **below Jev's only when h > 73.5%**.
* At **0% cache hit, Jev is cheaper at every input size** — its per-token price beats the LLM's cache-miss price and it pays nothing for output. There is no crossover, so R3's row "0% → >7,071 token" is wrong in sign, and its printed per-call table contradicts it ($0.000063 < $0.000297 at 1,500 tokens).
* With 120 output tokens, the crossover exists only for h > 73.5%: **≈4,250 tokens at h = 0.85**, **≈2,275 at h = 0.95** — right order of magnitude, wrong endpoints.

The qualitative claim ("the cost advantage is not a constant and can vanish as state grows") survives **only conditionally on a high cache-hit rate** — i.e. it depends on precisely the quantity R5 §4.4 #16 says "必须实测…不得沿用 R15 的 85% 假设" (must be measured; R15's 85% assumption must not be reused). And note the sensitivity direction: **more reasoning tokens moves the crossover down** (Jev's advantage appears earlier), while **fewer cache hits removes it entirely**. R5 must publish the crossover as a function of (h, effort), with the h ≤ 73.5% corner stated as "no crossover exists", not as a number.

### 2.3 Omitted terms — four with real magnitude or real sign risk

**(a) Reasoning/output-token cost in thinking mode — omitted, and it flips the headline.** R3 §1 deliberately kept "R15 的全部 token 量假设不变" (all of R15's token assumptions unchanged) and replaced only prices. But R15's "≈120 out/step" judgment assumption is the **one** token assumption that is effort-dependent, and R2 §A.1 #3 says in terms: default thinking is ON, reasoning tokens are billed at the output price, and effort "可能是一个数量级" (may be an order of magnitude) — *"必须纳入扫描，而不是固定在默认档"* (must be swept, not fixed at the default). With reasoning tokens R billed at $0.60/M:

| configuration | $/judgment | calls per $0.10 | per checkpoint (120) |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-thinking (R=0), 85% hit | $0.00010958 | 913 | 7.6 |
| R=800, 85% hit | $0.00058958 | 169 | 1.41 |
| **R=1,500, 85% hit** | **$0.00100958** | **99** | **0.83 → budget-bound** |
| R=1,500, 0% hit | $0.00112500 | 89 | 0.74 → budget-bound |

So "even in the most pessimistic case $0.10 buys 337 ≥ 2.8× the checkpoints" (R5 §0; R3 §4) is true **only for a non-thinking, short-output judge** — a configuration R5 names nowhere for A1. R5 §2 fact 10 pins non-thinking for A5c only (*"自洽采样臂必须走非思考模式"*), so the design as written has A5c cheap-and-non-thinking and A1 unpinned, and the cost conclusion inherits whichever A1 turns out to be. This is the strongest single omission on the dollar axis, because it can restore a real budget difference *and* it is already flagged as a first-order lever in the registry the team says it obeys.

**(b) Cache-WRITE pricing and the cache-ordering contradiction — omitted, and structural.** The registry (R2 §A; R16 §B.1 symbols) prices exactly three things: `p_hit`, `p_miss`, `p_out`. There is **no cache-write or cache-storage term anywhere**, so R3 cannot have included one and the paper must either reconcile one invoice against logged usage (R16 §B.5 #13) or declare write-side pricing as an unmodelled, assumed-zero term. More damaging: the 85% hit rate is not merely unmeasured, it is **inconsistent with the frozen serializer**. R16 §B.4 grounds the 50× discount in a layout where "**stable prefix = state**, variable suffix = question"; R15 §0.3 freezes the opposite for Laya's benefit — "newest-first ordering … so that an end-truncating judge (Laya) loses the OLDEST material first … **This is a fairness decision, not a favour to Laya**". Newest-first puts the *changing* text at the head, so the longest common prefix between consecutive judge prompts is short and the achievable hit rate on the state portion collapses toward the static instruction block. **R5 has therefore chosen, for the typed judge's benefit, an ordering that destroys the LLM judge's cache economics, and then priced the LLM judge as if it kept them.** Neither R15 nor R5 notices that these two decisions are in tension. Direction: this makes the LLM arm look *cheaper* than it is, i.e. it *understates* the typed judges' cost advantage; but it also means the pre-registered "no dollar difference" (TOST) is a property of an assumed cache rate, not a measurement.
*Minimal fix:* run both serializer orders (R15 §0.3 already budgets the ablation) and publish the joint table {order} × {measured hit rate, LLM $/call, Laya `blind_frac`}, with the primary order's cost consequence pre-registered. Note also the arithmetic inconsistency: R3 prices a 1,500-token judgment while R5 §4.2 #5 caps state at 450 tokens and R5 §5.1 caps the F2 decision unit at ≤190 — the per-call token volume was never reconciled with the design's own budget, and the direction of that error is to make the LLM look 2–3× more expensive than the design implies. Since R5 §5.4 freezes a token count in every `SNAP[run][t]`, the correct move is to compute cost from those frozen counts and the logged `usage` fields of R5 §4.4 #15–16, not from R15's template volumes.

**(c) Human review `h` and engineering amortisation — omitted, and each can dominate.** R16 §B.3 puts `P_human · (human minutes · h)` into `C_task`; §B.4 warns "omit human review or cache hits and the curve is fiction"; §B.6 makes `h ∈ {0, low, high}` a pre-registered grid row; §B.5 #11 calls `h` a *team policy parameter, swept*. R3 §1's price table has four rows, none of them human; R5 §4.4 has eighteen constraints, none of them human. Engineering is worse: R15 §6.2 books $1,500–3,000 of engineering, which after the 100× grader correction (§2.3(d)) is **69–82% of the study's total budget on R15's own prices (75–86% on R3's corrected prices)**, while the entire per-run judgment budget is $0.013. Amortised over the full 576-run design that is $2.60–5.20 per run — **200–400× the LLM judgment budget**. Nor is it arm-symmetric: the typed arms need a sidecar with health check and auto-restart (R5 §4.1 #3, because "sidecar 静默退出"), a local tokenizer-based acceptance gate (R5 §4.2 #4, #7, because the plugin `laya_plan` is "已损坏"), dual-host handling (R5 §4.1 #1), and harness-side input validation (R5 §4.2 #9) — the LLM arm needs the API client it already has for generation. R16 §B.1 has no symbol for integration amortisation; `λ` covers only per-round-trip orchestration, and R5 drops even `λ` from its constraint list. Direction: favours the LLM arm, and by an order of magnitude more than the whole token-economics difference.
*Minimal fix:* two-tier cost reporting — tier (i) marginal $/run at frozen token counts (R3's table, corrected per §2.2–§2.3), tier (ii) fully-loaded $/run including engineering amortisation at the pre-registered run count and `h` swept over R16 §B.6's grid. The claim "cost is not the constraint" is licensed for tier (i) only, and the abstract must say which tier it means.

**(d) Retries/timeouts, egress/compliance, grader.** Retries are R16 §B.6's "hidden budget sink" and R16 §A.5's "uncharged samples = free accuracy"; R5 specifies no timeout and no retry policy for any arm (§5.4). Egress/data-residency has no registry line at all: Jev is remote (R2 §C.1) and Laya is local, so a compliance term would favour the local judge — it is near-zero for this study's synthetic state and must simply be **declared as an excluded term**, since locality is not the same property as typedness. The grader is common across arms and therefore cannot bias the arm contrast — but R15's row is doubly wrong and that matters for the budget narrative: the count 69,120 is 120 × 576, so *"5 judges ×"* in the label is spurious; and **$3,802 = 100.01 × the $38.02** implied by R15's own assumptions, so R5 §8 item 1 is answered — it is a 100× decimal error. Consequently R15's "single most important budget fact" (*"the dominant cost is measurement, not judgment: ~55% of the total and ~17× the entire judgment budget"*) is false under R15's own arithmetic: gold ≈ $38 vs judgment ≈ $409 → 9%, and 0.09×.

**(e) The pre-registered TOST is close to vacuous.** R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 1 keeps a two-panel frontier and pre-registers *"TOST 等价性检验并报告「美元轴无显著差异」"*. But the dollar axis is **saturated**: at corrected prices, k=1 at every checkpoint costs ≈$0.0131/run and k=8 costs ≈$0.105/run, while R15 §1.6's sweep grid is B ∈ {$0.02, $0.05, $0.10, $0.25, $0.50, $1.00, $2.50} — six of seven points above the k=8 saturation point. Every arm will therefore achieve 1.0 checks/step at six of seven grid points and the "frontier" collapses to a point. Equivalence on an axis where both arms are pinned at the ceiling is a design artefact, not a finding; and the informative regime lies two orders of magnitude below `B_lo` (**≈$0.001–$0.02/run**). Note also that R5 keeps a frontier figure that R2 §C.5 forbids drawing until Jev is measured: *"⚠️ … 在 R12 给出实测定价与耗时之前，等预算前沿图不得绘制"* (before R12 supplies measured pricing and latency, the equal-budget frontier figure must not be drawn) — and my probe confirms Jev is still a mock.

**VERDICT: sound-with-fix.** The demotion is directionally right for *marginal API token spend in a non-thinking, short-output configuration at 120 checkpoints* — and only there. The derivation omits reasoning-token cost (which flips the headline at effort ≥ ~1,500 output tokens), engineering amortisation (60–85% of the real budget, asymmetric in the LLM's favour), human review `h` (a required R16 grid row), cache-write pricing and the serializer-ordering conflict, retries/timeouts, and egress. **Minimal fix:** (i) relabel the claim as tier-(i) marginal cost at a *named* effort level; (ii) rebuild the cost table from the frozen `SNAP` token counts and logged `usage`; (iii) reinstate R16 §B.6's grid verbatim (time-of-day, h, effort, typed-price multiplier, retry rate, human rate) and publish break-even *prices* in §B.6's form rather than a point estimate; (iv) sweep the frontier at B ∈ {$0.002, $0.005, $0.01, $0.02, $0.05} where the LLM arm is actually budget-bound, or drop the dollar panel to a table and state it is a null by design (R2 §C.5's prohibition applies until Jev is live).

## 3. Q3 — The pre-registered prompt band (R16 §A.2.3, gates 3–4)

**What R16 demands.** *"Pre-register **four** prompt configurations and report all four as a band; the headline LLM number is the **best** configuration at equal spend, not the mean"* — P1 bare letters / P2 letters + descriptions / P3 P2 + boundary + `uncertain` permitted / P4 P2 with `reasoning_effort ∈ {none, low, high, max}`; report min/median/max accuracy and ECE; and *"**The typed services get the analogous band** (option order, option count up to the documented accuracy cliff, and — for Laya — the `laya_plan`-style pre-check on/off). Anything less is asymmetric."* R16 §D.5 makes it control #1 of four and states "all four are required; any one alone is insufficient"; §A.7 prints it as gates 3 and 4.

**Does R5 bind itself? No — not anywhere.** R5 contains no P1–P4, no min/median/max reporting rule, and no "best variant is the headline" clause (search-verified). What R5 does instead is the opposite of a band in three places:

* R5 §5.3 freezes **one** A1 prompt: *"同一 LLM，结构化同题（JSON，含 `label` + `prob`）"*;
* R5 §4.3 #12 freezes **one** option order for the typed side — *"冻结一个规范 criteria 顺序并全程使用（置换会移动 confidence 达 0.111）"* — which is the typed side's *anti*-band;
* R5 §4.2 #8 freezes option cardinality at ≤10 (Path A) / ≤3 (Path B) rather than banding it up to the documented cliff.

R5 does grant the typed side one robustness band (Path A main + Path B control, §4.1 #1) — which makes the asymmetry sharper, not weaker: the typed judge gets a two-host band *and* a pre-check workaround, while the LLM judge gets a single frozen prompt.

**Exposure, stated precisely.** This is the one objection R16 §D.5 predicts by name — *"Your LLM baseline is a straw man. A better-prompted LLM judge, given the same budget, would match or beat your typed judges — and your result is a prompt artifact"* — and R5 has removed the control that exists to defuse it while retaining the other three (A1, A5b/A5c, and, in R16, the negative-result commitment). A reviewer does not need to believe the objection to reject on it: they need only observe that the design *pre-registered a rule that the best LLM variant is the headline* in R16 and then failed to collect any variants.

**A second-order defect the team has not noticed.** "Headline = best of four variants" is itself a selection step. If it is applied after seeing test results, the LLM arm's headline is inflated by the maximum of four noisy estimates and Gate 1's Holm family does not account for it. R5 already owns the correct machinery: the P5 calibration corpus runs **before any test run** and is used to freeze τ\*. The band's headline variant must be selected the same way — **on the P5 corpus, frozen before unblinding** — which costs no new infrastructure.

**Cost of the fix.** On the replay plane: 1,000 items × 4 variants ≈ 4,000 calls ≈ **$0.44**; plus the typed-side band (2 orders × 2 cardinalities) ≈ another dollar. No new generation, no new task families.

**VERDICT: unsound.** R5 does not bind itself to R16 §A.2.3/§A.7 gates 3–4 in any form, and the missing control is the design's own top-ranked rejection risk. **Minimal fix:** add to R5 §5.3 a single row *"A1-band (P1–P4)"*, run on the measurement plane on every item; add the typed-side band (option order × cardinality {≤8, 11–20} × Path A/B); pre-register that the headline LLM number is the best variant **selected on the P5 calibration corpus**, with min/median/max reported for all four; and replace R5 §4.3 #12's justification (see §5.6).

## 4. Q4 — Is A1's three-confound differencing sound?

### 4.1 The part that is sound, and I say so plainly

R15 §0.2's guarantee is unusually strong for this literature and it is the reason the design is worth auditing rather than dismissing: the snapshot is partitioned into a **STATE payload** ("identical bytes for every judge") and a **QUESTION block** ("the same question text, option set and rubric for every judge asked that question. **No judge gets a longer or friendlier state**"). Combined with R5 §5.4's exhaustive offline replay ("run 结束后**全部判定器在冻结快照上回放**"), this genuinely removes from the *measurement* plane: (1) mere re-asking, (2) format, (3) dose/schedule — because every judge answers byte-identical state at every checkpoint. R15 §1.2's argument that without A1 "the paper's headline result is a restatement of test–retest reliability" is correct, and R15 §1.2's claim that the error "is not detectable post hoc" is also correct. A1 is the right instrument and R5 §5.3 correctly calls it *"不可谈判的守门人"* (non-negotiable gatekeeper).

### 4.2 Residual confound #1 — self-judgment (not removed, and cheaply removable)

R15 §1.2 states the estimand and the claim: *"With A1 in hand, A2/A3 differ from it in **exactly one** respect — the identity of the network answering `Q(ck)`."* That is false in a second respect. In A1 the network is judging **its own output**; in A2/A3 the network judges a *stranger's* output (the generator's). Self-preference / self-enhancement is documented as an LLM-judge bias and is measured in the literature R16 itself cites (R16 §C.1, [arXiv:2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819) links preference to the low perplexity of one's own output). So `β = P(catch | judge X) − P(catch | same LLM)` contains a self-vs-other term of unknown sign, and the design's own words make it worse: R15 §1.2 asserts the identity confound is *isolated* when it is only *netted*.

This matters for the paper's central contrast: if self-judgment inflates "accept" (self-enhancement) then A1 under-detects and every typed-judge gain is overstated; if it deflates "accept" (the model can read its own reasoning traces) then A1 over-detects and the typed judges are understated. The design cannot tell which, and the *direction decides the headline*.

**The fix is free and the design already owns it.** The measurement plane replays on frozen snapshots, so add **A1x: the same LLM, the same question, a *foreign* run's snapshot** (matched by task family and horizon level). That decomposes `β` into (self-vs-other) + (species) at ~$0.11 per 1,000 items and zero new generation. Without A1x, R15 §1.2's "exactly one respect" must be rewritten to "two respects, one of which we do not identify".

### 4.3 Residual confound #2 — the calibration comparison gives the LLM arm a strictly poorer instrument

R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 2 makes calibration a first-class claim — *"校准与可靠度曲线：**≥1000**，因为 ECE 是本文的核心部署结论之一"* (calibration and reliability curves: ≥1000, because ECE is one of the paper's core deployment conclusions) — and R5 §4.3 #10 requires that the typed judges "一律报**完整分布**" (always report the full distribution) for `choice`/`score`. A1, as frozen in R5 §5.3 / R15 §1.2, emits `{"label": <one of the 8 options>, "prob": <float>}` — a top-label scalar, with *"no free-text field … no rationale field, no chain-of-thought field"*. R16 §A.2.1 requirement **R3** is explicit that the LLM arm can and must do better: *"`p_all` gives a probability for EVERY option key; entries must sum to 1.00 (±0.02)"*, and R16 §A.2.2 schedules four readouts (verbalized, logit, sample-consistency, temperature-scaled) with *"both sides get the same repair budget"*.

So the paper's designated core deployment conclusion currently compares **full distributions from the typed judges against a single verbalized probability from the LLM** — the LLM arm is denied both `p_all` and the logit readout that the registry verifies it has (R2 §A: `logprobs`, `top_logprobs` 0–20). Any ECE/reliability advantage for the typed side is then partly an artefact of instrument asymmetry, not of calibration quality. Symmetrically, A1 is never temperature-scaled while R16 §A.2.2 rule 4 requires the same held-out repair for both sides.

### 4.4 Residual confound #3 — the "same question" guarantee is a snapshot guarantee, not a wire guarantee

Four ways the *delivered* inputs differ even when the snapshot bytes do not:

1. **Wrapper.** Each judge receives its own framing: R16 §A.2.1's SYSTEM/RULES block (~200 tokens of format law, `boundary`, `uncertain`, JSON) for A1, versus Laya's own rendering. The guarantee covers the payload, not the prompt; R5 nowhere requires the per-judge wire templates to be published side by side. A reviewer cannot verify "no judge got a friendlier question" without them.
2. **Option text.** Laya compresses option text once its planner is tight — R2 §B.4 measures 49 tokens/label at 2–10 options versus 25–34 at 15–20 with *"压缩: 是"* — so above the compression point A1 and A3 answer **different questions** while the snapshot is identical.
3. **The option-token specifications are mutually unsatisfiable.** R15 §0.4: *"**≤48 tokens per option**, **≤160 tokens of option text per question**"*; R14 §4.1/§7 G1: *"≤4 options × ≤38 tok"*, *"≤192 tok/question"*; R5 §4.2 #8: *"`tightest_option_tokens_each < 49` 视为**设计失败**"* (fewer than 49 tokens per option = design failure). R5 requires ≥49 where R15 permits ≤48 and R14 supplies ≤38 — **as written, every R15-conforming question is an R5 design failure**, and R5's ≥49 floor is itself a Laya-protective rule (it keeps Laya out of the compressed-option regime) that changes the question for *all* judges. R5 §5.1's F2 decision unit (≤130 + ≤60 tokens) cannot host 8 options × ≥49 tokens at all.
4. **Ask rate / dose.** R15 §1's arm table gives A1 ask rate *"every step"* and A2/A3 *"fixed schedule"*. On the control plane A1 is therefore dosed more heavily than the typed arms; on the replay plane dose is equalised by construction. R5 §5.5 then names the primary endpoint *"`T90` … **在匹配顺序往返下**"* (at matched sequential round-trips) while R5 §5.3 contains **no schedule factor at all** — so the endpoint's conditioning phrase is unimplementable as specified, and the two planes are not distinguished in the endpoint's name. (V5 §0.7 finds the same plane ambiguity from the reproducibility side; the arm-design consequence is that "matched round-trips" needs R15 §2's S0–S4 factor restored, or must be struck from the endpoint and replaced by an explicit plane name.)

### 4.5 Residual confound #4 — failure handling is asymmetric, and A1's is undefined

* A0: *"extraction failures default to `accept` and are logged"* (R15 §1.1) — the LLM prose judge's failure mode silently **suppresses** detection.
* Jev: *"`unknown` → log as judge-absent (a **no-verdict**, never imputed as `accept`)"* (R15 §1.3) — and A4 escalates on `insufficient | undecided | unknown`, so the typed judge's abstention **buys it another call** (R5 §5.3 A4 e3).
* A1: **unspecified**, despite being the floor for every claim. Whether an invalid A1 JSON counts as `accept`, as censored, or as a re-ask is a hidden degree of freedom that moves the headline, and R16 §A.2.1 already anticipates the failure (`response_format` plus `finish_reason == "length"` → *invalid*).
* Timeouts: R5 specifies none for any arm, though the design's own methodology exemplar (R2 §C.4) froze "timeout 10 s, zero retries".

**VERDICT: sound-with-fix.** The three-confound argument is valid **for the payload-level estimand it claims** on the measurement plane, and the byte-identical-state + exhaustive-replay construction is the design's best feature. But "exactly one respect" is overstated (self-judgment is unidentified), the calibration claim compares unequal instruments (`p_all` and logit denied to the LLM arm), the parity guarantee is not enforced at the wire, the option-token specs contradict each other, and A1's invalid-output policy is undefined. **Minimal fix:** add A1x (foreign-state replay); give A1 `p_all` + the logit variant + symmetric temperature scaling; publish a prompt-parity table (per-judge wire template + hash + *effective* option text and token count); collapse the three option specs into one table and delete R5's ≥49-token rule; pre-register one invalid/timeout policy applied identically to every arm (no-verdict → censored → one budget-charged re-ask); and put the plane name (`_replay` / `_online`) in every endpoint label.

## 5. Q5 — Asymmetries favouring the typed judges that the research question does not justify

Each row: what the asymmetry is, the text that creates it, whether it is justified, and the fix. I include two asymmetries that favour the *LLM* arm (rows 10–11) because a fairness audit that only looks one way is itself unfair.

### 5.1 The typed judge's characteristic failure mode is designed out of the primary matrix (favours typed; unjustified)

R5 §4.2 #5: *"**硬上限 450 state token/问**；`state_tokens ≤ 512 − head_tokens − 1`；安全区 ≤400."* R5 §5.2: *"载体宽度、**token 数**、片段位置、选项位置、族混合**全部固定**."* R14 §7 gate G1: *"every item: `laya_plan(strict=true)` clean; state ≤512 tok (target ≤497) … **reject item**, report drop rate"*, and R14 §1.3's capability matrix marks *"multi-span state > ~480 tokens"* as **"no — tail silently dropped"** for Laya. R2 §B.3 measures the silent window at ≈2,850–3,148 characters (≈450–500 real tokens) with `fits: true`, no `truncated`, no warning, and bit-identical output freezes across 2,150 characters.

Consequence: in the primary battery **no Laya call is truncated**, so R5 §5.5's retained metric *"`token_ratio(t)` 生存曲线（Laya 可用性随 horizon）"* is a constant 0, and R15 §3.5's `P(wrong | truncated) / P(wrong | in-window)` — the paper's most decision-relevant quantity — has no sample. The only instruments left are R13's single-slot probes (n = 202 calls, one host, one checkpoint) and R14 §4.3's carrier-lift probe (secondary, 100 paired instances, two item classes) — neither of which is the primary experiment. Meanwhile the paper's title is *"成本不是约束，**静默退化**才是"* (cost is not the constraint, silent degradation is). **The typed judge's worst failure mode is suppressed by design while its cost advantage is asserted; that is the definition of an unjustified asymmetry, and it also invalidates the headline.** Fix: make state length a **designed factor** with an over-window level (e.g. `state_len ∈ {≤400, ≈1,500, >3,000 tokens}` crossed with the horizon dial), keeping `laya_plan(strict=true)` as the acceptance gate *only* for the in-window stratum; pre-register the over-window stratum as primary evidence for S3 rather than as a robustness chapter (R5 §7 P10 currently files it there).

### 5.2 Batching is an affordance granted to the LLM arm and withheld from the typed arms (favours typed; unjustified by R5's own logic)

R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 1 accepts R13's constraint 22 — *"一个批处理另一个不批处理的比较不是比较"* (a comparison in which one batches and the other does not is not a comparison) — and uses it to keep A5b. But the constraint is **symmetric**, and R5 §5.3 gives batching only to the LLM (A5b ≤8 checkpoints/call). The typed arm has no batched row at all, even though the registry measures Laya's per-question amortisation directly (R2 §B.1: 30.0 ms at batch 1 → 6.0 at 5 → 3.9 at 10 → **3.2 ms at 20**), and R16 §A.0.1's mirrored ladder requires the typed side to get "k repeated calls, aggregate probabilities; **plus batch-q questions per state**". R5 §5.5 partially compensates by *reporting* serial vs parallelisable cost curves — but reporting the parallelisability of an affordance the typed arm was never allowed to use is not the same as running it. Fix: add A3b (Laya, ≤20 questions/call) and A2b (Jev `rank`-style batching, R15 §1.3) as explicit rows and report the {batching × judge} factorial; then the round-trip claim is measured rather than assumed.

### 5.3 Escalation exists only for the typed judges (favours typed; unjustified)

R5 §5.3 A4 is defined as *"自适应升级（先廉价后升级）"* (adaptive escalation: cheap first, then escalate) with Laya/Jev first; R5 §5.3's own justification is *"**Laya 自带 `act_costs.escalate` 语义**，且 `esc(t)` 随 horizon 上升本身是结果"*. The rule set (R15 §1.5) fires on Jev's `insufficient/undecided/unknown` and on Laya's low probability — but **the LLM arm's own `prob` never buys it a second call**, and there is no LLM self-escalation arm (e.g. non-thinking → thinking, or low effort → high effort, on low `p`). So the paper's §9 "composition architecture" result — `esc(t)` rising with horizon — is a curve only one species can produce, and the abstract's deployment recommendation ("cheap-judge-first is deployable") is supported by an experiment in which only the cheap judge was allowed to be first. Prior art for the missing control is already in R16 §C.3 ([Confident Adaptive Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07061), [AutoMix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963), [Bayesian self-escalation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24087)), so a reviewer will ask for it by name. Fix: add **A4′ = LLM self-escalation** with τ calibrated on the same P5 corpus and the retry charged to the arm; report `esc(t)` for both species on one axis.

### 5.4 The finetuning branch gives the typed judge in-domain labels and gives the LLM arm nothing (favours typed; unjustified)

R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 3: *"增加一个微调分支：在**独立训练划分**上微调 `laya-typed-decisions`，在留出集上评测"* — i.e. the typed judge may be trained on the task distribution, with the stated goal "can specialisation push 421M to frontier level". R5 says nothing about giving the LLM arm an equivalent adaptation budget, yet R16 §A.0 rung 6b exists precisely to force that disclosure: *"**Gets training labels** — the typed judges are zero-shot; note the asymmetry explicitly"*, and R16 §A.2.2 rule 4 insists "both sides get the same repair budget". A finetuned-vs-zero-shot comparison is not the paper's claim, and a hostile reviewer will read it as one. Fix: make adaptation an explicit factor — `adaptation ∈ {zero-shot, in-domain}` × `judge ∈ {LLM, Laya}` — where the LLM's in-domain adaptation is few-shot exemplars (and optionally a LoRA) drawn from **the same labelled split**; report the 2×2 and never pool; if only the typed side can be adapted within budget, print the asymmetry on the same line as every adapted number, per R16 §A.6.

### 5.5 Latency is now the primary axis, and it is timed unequally (favours typed; unjustified)

R3 §4 demotes dollars and R15 §1.6 nominates latency/round-trips as the real asymmetry, so *latency quality is now load-bearing*. Three defects:

* **Contention.** R15 §6.2 runs generation at *"8 并行流"* (8 parallel streams) against the same endpoint while the judgment calls are issued inside that loop. The LLM judge's measured latency therefore includes queueing against its own generator's traffic, while Laya's 34 ms/140 ms (R2 §B.1) are idle-host numbers. Fix: time judge latency in a dedicated no-contention block and report contended/uncontended separately; if the deployment claim is about in-loop latency, keep the contended number but state that the comparison is contended-vs-idle.
* **Warm-up asymmetry.** R5 §4.1 #3 records Laya's cold starts and excludes them from the mean ("冷启动 389.9 ms / 4935.9 ms vs 热 37 ms，混入会污染延迟"), while nothing excludes the remote judges' cold conditions — first-call cache population (at 50× the hit price, R2 §A.1 #2) and TLS/connection setup — from *their* means. R16 gate 12 says "warm-up before timing" for every arm. Fix: pre-warm each remote judge with discarded calls before any timed block; report p50/p95 post-warm-up for all arms.
* **Network not decomposed.** R5 §4.1 #2 pins Laya's device; nothing pins the network path of the two remote judges (Jev's registry latency is third-party with "区域未标", R2 §C.3). A "typed judges don't consume a sequential round-trip" headline that compares a local GPU call to two remote calls must decompose compute vs RTT (a null request to each endpoint), and must state the deployment assumption (co-located sidecar) that makes the claim true.

### 5.6 Option order is frozen to protect a statistic R5 forbids using — while the LLM arm's position bias goes uncontrolled (favours typed; internally inconsistent)

R5 §4.3 #12: *"**冻结一个规范 criteria 顺序**并全程使用（置换会移动 confidence 达 0.111）"*. The stated reason is protection of `confidence` — but R5 §4.3 #10 one constraint earlier says *"**`confidence` 永不用于门控、阈值、路由**"* and permits only `noul`'s `band`. So the design freezes the stimulus in order to stabilise a number it has banned, while R16 §A.2.1 requires the opposite for the LLM arm: *"**Option-order randomisation.** Present options in randomised order per item (with the order logged) and, if budget allows, run both orders as a position control."* The measured Laya order effect is on `p`/`confidence` only (R2 §B.6: argmax stable, p moves ≤0.0515, confidence 0.1107) — so freezing the order buys Laya almost nothing (its argmax is stable) and costs the design its only control for the LLM's documented position bias. Fix: randomise per item with the order logged; keep the canonical order for the Laya *stability* analysis; run both orders as a pre-registered position control for **all** judges, mirroring R2 §C.4 #2's alternating-order discipline.

### 5.7 Smaller asymmetries, and what they cost

* **Repeats.** R5 §4.3 #13 restricts repeats to near-ties for the typed judges on determinism grounds (R2 §B.6: bit-identical on repeat), while A5c gives the LLM k=8 at every checkpoint. R5 §3 ⚖️ conflict 2 discloses this asymmetry honestly ("这个不对称是真实的，且对类型化判定器有利，应在论文中明说"), which is to its credit. **Fix:** give the typed arm its own variance-reduction analogue — R15 §2's depth-`d` question ensembling (base question + d−1 follow-ups of other types) — and pre-register it, so "the typed judge cannot buy variance reduction" is a measured statement rather than an assumption.
* **Option cardinality.** The ceiling (8 in R15 §0.4, ≤10 in R5 §4.2 #8) is set by Laya's compression onset (R2 §B.4: Path A compresses from 11 options), not by Jev's or the LLM's capability; Jev's only documented advantage is at high cardinality (R2 §B.8, third-party: Banking77, >20 options). R5 §7 P10 files 11–20 options under "robustness". **Fix:** make cardinality part of the pre-registered band (§3) so the interaction is estimable, per R16 §A.2.3's typed-side band requirement.
* **Cold-start costing.** R3's per-call Laya cost excludes the 25–35 s sidecar start (R15 §6.1) amortised per session, and remote judges' handshake costs are excluded too. Trivial in dollars; must be named as an excluded term so the cost table is closed.
* **`revise` semantics are undefined, which makes effective horizon arm-dependent.** R5 §5.3 A0–A5b all use `revise` to re-prompt the same generator under the verdict's label (R15 §0.1), and R15 §2 logs `Δgen` as a covariate — but **nothing states whether a `revise` consumes one of the `N_step` task steps or extends the run**. If it consumes a step, arms that detect more cover less task ground inside the same 120 steps, so the drift/`T90`/`LEAD` metrics are computed against a gold reference trajectory (`REF[T][t]`, R15 §3.1) that no longer aligns step-for-step; if the run extends, horizon itself becomes a function of judge aggressiveness. Fix: define `t` as the task-progress index, pre-register that `revise` does not advance it, and report generation calls (not task steps) as the exposure covariate.
* **No judge-off arm.** (V5 §4.2 C5.) R15 §2's S0 "judge-never" is not in R5 §5.3's arm table, so the design has no arm in which no verdict influences control flow — which is the only clean attribution of "judgment helps at all". Fold S0 back in as a schedule level, not an arm.

**VERDICT: unsound.** The typed-judge-favouring asymmetries are not cosmetic: §5.1 removes the phenomenon the paper is titled after, §5.2/§5.3 withhold from the typed arm the very affordances R5 uses to defend the LLM arm (batching) and to headline the paper (escalation), and §5.4 changes what "typed judge" means for one arm only. Each fix is cheap; none requires new task families or new models; most are replay-only.

## 6. Q6 — Does the design imply the claims it forbids?

R5 §6 lists the forbidden set: *"**不得主张** = 「Jev/Laya 优于 LLM」、新范式、普遍校准优越、新的级联架构、无条件的「每任务更便宜」"*. Five separate places imply forbidden content anyway.

**(a) The analysis plan is the machinery of the forbidden claim.** Gate 1 (R15 §5.4, retained in R5 §5.4's hierarchy) is *"one endpoint (`T90` lead time, matched judgment dollars) × **the five planned contrasts** (A1 vs A0; A2 vs A1; A3 vs A1; A4 vs A1; A5b/A5c vs A2/A3). Holm–Bonferroni."* Every one of those five is a **superiority contrast between species**, on a comparative primary endpoint, with family-wise error control. The paper can label itself a characterization study, but its pre-registered primary analysis is a ranking test, and the reader will read the ranking. The forbidden claim is prohibited in words while its statistical form is pre-registered. **De-risk:** either (i) state the primary estimand as a characterization quantity with a pre-registered equivalence margin on the judge axis (so a null is a positive finding), or (ii) keep the superiority family but print the confound list — typedness vs **locality** vs **capacity** (421M vs frontier) vs **zero-shot-ness** vs **determinism** — in the same paragraph as every headline contrast, since the arm set cannot separate them (Jev, the only remote typed judge and the only one that isolates typedness from locality, is mock-blocked).

**(b) The arm layout decides the result before the data arrives.** The primary typed judge is A3 = zero-shot Laya, which the team's own registry measures at 0.362 against a 0.461 majority-class baseline (R2 §B.7). The primary endpoint is detection lead time. Therefore the pre-registered primary result is *"the frontier LLM detects earlier than the near-chance local judge"* — a finding the design then forbids itself from generalising in the *other* direction but which will be read as a species verdict regardless, because the arms are laid out as a species duel and the endpoint ranks them. **De-risk:** pre-register the negative-result commitment R16 §D.5 control 4 demands and R5 dropped — *"if the best-prompted LLM arm dominates the frontier at equal spend, the paper reports that as the finding, with the same prominence"* — and state in the abstract, with the T90 number, that A3 is zero-shot and A2 is mock.

**(c) The title asserts a class claim from one artifact.** *"《长线任务中**判别式判定器**的真实代价：成本不是约束，静默退化才是》"* generalises to a class (discriminative judges) from (i) one encoder checkpoint, (ii) one host, (iii) a mechanism R13 explicitly declines to explain, and (iv) a *software defect* — R2 §B.3 shows the window is produced by `_CHARS_PER_TOKEN = 4.0` × `_SAFETY = 1.15` in `planning.py`. The subtitle (*"一个本地 421M 非自回归判定模型与一个前沿生成模型在相同条目上的过程级对照"*) is a size-and-species headline of exactly the kind R16 §D.4 #1 forbids. On the parameter count: R2 §B.2 says the count is unconfirmed and *"论文不得写 421M"*; V5 measured 421,293,830 from the `model.safetensors` header, which makes the number citable **with its derivation** — R5's subtitle carries no derivation. **De-risk:** scope the title to the artifact and the deployment ("measured on this build, this host"), cite the parameter count with V5's derivation in a footnote, and file the upstream report (V5 §5) so the negative result has a citable object.

**(d) The headline claim is mislabelled as measurement.** R5 §0's table is headed **"实测裁决"** (measured verdict) and its first row is *"钱不是约束（R3）"* — but R3 is a model built on R15's *assumed* token volumes, an *assumed* 85% cache-hit rate that R5 §4.4 #16 itself forbids reusing, and a *fixed* non-thinking output-token count (§2.3). R2's own rule is that unverified items must not enter the text (*"**未核实（NOT VERIFIED）** 的项目不得写入正文"*). **De-risk:** relabel the row "model-derived, conditional on R5 §4.4 #16 and the effort sweep", and make the title's *"成本不是约束"* conditional on the tier-(i)/tier-(ii) distinction of §2.3(c) — at the study level cost is demonstrably **the** constraint (engineering is ≈70–85% of the corrected budget), which a reviewer with a calculator will point out using R15 §6.2's own line items.

**(e) The retained metrics keep the forbidden reading alive.** R5 §5.5 keeps *"`token_ratio(t)`"* (degenerate per §5.1), *"成本-完成曲线"*, and R5's appendix §6 keeps *"等预算前沿 + **TOST**"* — i.e. a cost-superiority figure and an equivalence test on an axis that cannot discriminate (§2.3(e)), under a title that says cost is not the constraint. A figure that shows no cost difference, drawn on a saturated axis, next to a title claiming cost is irrelevant, is the shape of a paper that is arguing for the forbidden conclusion while disclaiming it. **De-risk:** replace the frontier figure with R16 §B.6's **break-even statement** (a price, not a frontier) and move the dollar material into a cost table with the sensitivity grid.

**VERDICT: unsound.** The SAFE/CONDITIONAL/forbidden claim *list* is disciplined (and V5 is right that it is the design's strongest asset), but the arm layout, the Gate-1 contrast family, the endpoint choice, the retained cost figure and the title together imply at least four forbidden or nearly-forbidden readings. **Minimal fix:** (i) reinstate R16 §D.5 control 4 verbatim as a pre-registered commitment; (ii) print the five-way confound (typedness/locality/capacity/zero-shot/determinism) beside every headline contrast and state that A2 is mock; (iii) scope the title to the artifact and drop the class-level noun; (iv) convert the frontier into a break-even price statement; (v) relabel R5 §0's "实测裁决" column for anything derived rather than measured.

## TOP 3 THINGS MOST LIKELY TO GET THIS PAPER REJECTED

1. **The title's claim has no instrument: "silent degradation" is designed out of the primary matrix** (state capped at 450 tokens, R5 §4.2 #5; token count fixed across the horizon dial, R5 §5.2; R14 gate G1 rejects any item that would truncate), so `token_ratio(t)` ≡ 0 and `P(wrong | truncated)` has no sample — while R5 §0 titles the paper after it, and this same choice is an unjustified asymmetry in the typed judge's favour.
 *One-line fix:* make state length a designed factor with an over-window level (`{≤400, ≈1,500, >3,000 tok}` × the horizon dial) and move the truncation evidence out of P10 robustness into the primary battery.
2. **The anti-straw-man controls R16 prints as gates 3–5 and 8 are absent — and restoring them costs about two dollars of frozen-snapshot replay** (no P1–P4 band, no logit readout, no `uncertain`/charged-retry, no A5b × A5c conjunction; `p_all` withheld from A1 while typed judges must report full distributions).
 *One-line fix:* add one row `A5*` (best P-band variant × `p_all` + logit × batch ≤8 × k=8 × `uncertain` + charged re-ask) on the measurement plane, select its headline variant on the P5 calibration corpus, and print R16 §A.7's gate table in the appendix.
3. **Every cost claim currently fails its own arithmetic or its own registry**: a 100× grader error ($3,802 = 100.01 × $38.02), an 85% cache-hit rate that the frozen newest-first serializer makes unattainable and an R2 §C.5 prohibition on drawing the frontier at all while Jev is mock, a 120-output-token assumption that thinking mode invalidates (99 calls per $0.10 < 120 checkpoints at R = 1,500), a non-reproducing Jev/LLM crossover whose 0%-cache row has the wrong sign, and engineering/human terms omitted while the title says "cost is not the constraint".
 *One-line fix:* rebuild the cost table from the frozen `SNAP` token counts plus logged `usage`, reinstating R16 §B.6's full sensitivity grid (time-of-day, `h`, effort, typed-price multiplier, retry rate, human rate) and publishing break-even prices instead of a frontier.
